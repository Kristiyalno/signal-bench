＋ Things worth trying on a mystery sound clip

  • Reverse it. Backmasked speech is the oldest trick — flip direction, then slow it down a little too.
  • Slow it way down or speed it way up. A voice sped 4× can sound like a tone; a tone slowed 4× can turn out to be a voice.
  • Check the spectrogram. Images and text are sometimes drawn directly into the frequency spectrum — invisible on the waveform, obvious once you look at frequency over time.
  • Isolate a channel. Stereo files can hide a message in just the left, just the right, or only in the difference between them (the side channel).
  • Listen for beeps. Regular short/long tones are almost always morse — run the decoder above.
  • Boost the gain and compress. Whispered or buried audio under a louder layer often becomes clear once amplified.
  • Don't forget the file itself. Metadata, filenames, and even the raw bytes of an audio file can carry a clue this toolkit won't show you — worth a look in a text/hex editor too.
